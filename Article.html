The Age of Artificial Intelligence Has Only Just Begun

From language models that write poetry to vision systems that detect cancer earlier than any doctor, AI has moved from science fiction to daily infrastructure faster than almost anyone predicted. But the real revolution, experts warn, is still ahead of us.

By Kasun Perera | June 2026 | 8 min read

From Curiosity to Infrastructure

Not long ago, "artificial intelligence" conjured images of robots and science fiction villains. Today it powers the search bar you typed into this morning, the spam filter that protected your inbox, and the traffic navigation app that rerouted you around that accident on the highway. AI has quietly become as foundational as electricity.

The shift accelerated sharply between 2022 and 2025, when large language models became publicly available and showed that machines could hold conversations, write software code, summarise legal documents, and even compose music — tasks that once seemed uniquely human. The question is no longer whether AI will transform industry. The question is: which industries will transform fastest?

"We are not building tools anymore. We are building collaborators — entities that think alongside us."

How Large Language Models Actually Work

At their core, large language models (LLMs) are statistical engines trained on enormous libraries of text. Given a prompt, they predict what word is most likely to come next, and then the word after that, and so on. What makes modern LLMs remarkable is scale: billions of learnable parameters tuned across trillions of words allow them to develop something that looks very much like understanding.

The training process involves two main stages. In pre-training, the model reads vast datasets — books, websites, scientific papers, code repositories — and adjusts its internal weights to become better at predicting text. In fine-tuning, human trainers guide the model toward helpful, accurate, and safe responses.
